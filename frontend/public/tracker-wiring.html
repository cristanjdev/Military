Armory Tracker Wiring

The tracker as it is built now: an ESP32 DevKit and a GPS module on a breadboard, three status lights, and a 21700 battery feeding the USB port through a charger and a 5 V boost board. Two resistors let the tracker measure its own battery. Pins match the tracker's firmware settings.

Hover or tap a wire to see what it connects.

Connections

Parts

  • 1×ESP32 DevKit V1, 30-pin (ESP-WROOM-32)
  • 1×GPS module, GY-NEO6MV2 or NEO-M8N, with ceramic antenna
  • 3×5 mm LED: green, blue, red
  • 3×330 Ω resistor (orange, orange, brown)
  • 1×Breadboard
  • 16×Jumper wires (male-to-male; male-to-female for the GPS)
  • 1×21700 Li-ion battery, 3.7 V 5,000 mAh, in a holder
  • 1×TP4056 charger board with protection
  • 1×Slide switch (on/off)
  • 1×5 V boost board with USB output
  • 2×100 kΩ resistor (brown, black, yellow)
  • 1×Short USB to micro-USB data cable

Before you power it

  • TX always goes to RX: GPS TX to D18, GPS RX to D19.
  • Solder the GPS header pins. Loose pins light the GPS LED but send no data.
  • The GPS runs on 3V3, not 5V.
  • Each LED's long leg (+) faces the resistor; the short leg (−) goes to ground.
  • Pin order is the common 30-pin DevKit V1. If your board differs, go by the labels printed next to each pin.
  • Keep the ceramic antenna facing the sky.
  • Set the boost board to 5.0 V with a multimeter before plugging in the ESP32.
  • Charge through the TP4056's USB-C port. Turn the switch off while charging.
  • D34 is on the pin row the breadboard covers, so solder its wire to the pin (or use the perfboard build). Then set HAS_BATTERY_MONITOR 1 and upload again; the system warns at 20% and 10%.
